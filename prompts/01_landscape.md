Given a target B2B company and its website, research its competitive landscape.

First, research the target company's product through primary product pages, documentation and other credible sources. Persist an evidence-grounded product_understanding: a summary, the core workflow, named capabilities with supporting source URLs, overall sources, and explicit uncertainties. Describe what the product actually enables and its relevant boundaries. Do not infer an ICP, buying situations, or stakeholders; those are later case-study findings.

Use that researched product understanding to identify and evaluate **direct competitors**. Explain how each specific competing product substitutes for relevant target capabilities or the same workflow. Similar terminology, vendor category membership, or broad industry overlap is insufficient. Exclude merely adjacent solutions. Do not impose an arbitrary number of competitors.

Present your findings in a table containing:
- Competitor company and relevant product
- Brief explanation of why it directly competes
- Supporting source URL(s)

Use credible evidence to establish competitive relevance. If a candidate's relevance is uncertain, distinguish it from confirmed competitors rather than presenting it as verified.

Identify the specific competing product or business line, even when its vendor has a broader portfolio. Explain the shared customer problem in the reason field. These saved product and reason fields define the scope for later case-study discovery; approval of a company does not approve every product it sells.

**Stop after presenting the competitive landscape.** I will approve the companies to include in our case-study research universe.

The target company itself will also be included in that universe. Do not begin case-study discovery until approval.

For each proposed product, put a concise qualification in reason: shared workflow and product overlap; current commercial activity with dated/source-supported evidence; and actual customer evidence (named case, testimonial or other credible usage evidence). Sources must support these statements. Distinguish “no public customer evidence found” from “no customers”: the former is uncertainty, never proof of the latter. A stale site or shared keywords alone cannot confirm active competition. Mark uncertain activity, customer evidence or overlap explicitly and use confidence=uncertain. Do not invent commercial or customer facts. Keep the proposal concise enough for the single competitor-universe approval gate.
